## 2. Why it works: 8 reasons
1. **A trusted character.** The rich, calm grandmother is an "old money" figure. She sounds like someone who has seen it all. #oldmoney is a big aesthetic trend, and her audience skews older and US-based (commenter names and hashtags #usa #newyork).
2. **The hook is the thumbnail.** Big white text with a black outline sits mid-frame on every Reel, and it's readable before the video even plays.
3. **The hooks threaten identity or promise secrets.** Five patterns carry almost all her views:
   - **"Only broke people ___"** (362K, 73K, 47K, 39K). The viewer thinks "am I broke?" and watches, or comments to argue.
   - **"Rich people / wealthy families never ___"** (199K, 31K, 17K)
   - **"There's a reason rich kids never ___"** (173K)
   - **"N signs / N things / N documents"** lists (157K, 114K, 73K, 68K, 56K)
   - **"___ doesn't want you to know"**, the forbidden-knowledge angle (68K)
4. **Topics are money rules plus status, not only math.** Trusts, LLCs, taxes, mortgages, bank deposits, what to say ("Never say $1,000", "Never answer 'what do you do'"), and raising kids. Etiquette and status topics make people send the video to family and friends.
5. **A comment keyword turns views into comments.** "Comment WEALTH / Blueprint" got 2.5K–6.4K comments per Reel. Without it: 172. Every keyword comment gets an auto-reply (each shows "View all 1 replies"). Comments and replies push the Reel to more people.
6. **The backdrops are aspirational and never repeat.** Private jet, Rolls-Royce, villa, yacht, estate garden: a new setting every Reel. It feeds the fantasy, and it also draws mocking comments that still count as engagement.
7. **Volume.** About one post a day, and only ~9% of Reels hit 100K+. Virality is a numbers game with a fixed format.
8. **A cheap product behind the free blueprint.** The Shopify store sells a **$9** ebook ("Why You Will Always Be Broke") and two **$47** ebooks (a family-trust guide and "Wealthy Mind Unlocked"), all shown as "sale" prices.

## 4. What Vance never copies (legal risk, and her own account got banned once)
- ❌ **Fake experience.** "Sixty years observing…", "My father paid my mother…", "the richest man I ever knew…". FTC 16 CFR 255 and 465 cover fictional endorsers, at up to ~$51,744 per violation. Vance speaks in the **third person about how wealthy families do things**. That's how most of her top hooks are written anyway ("Rich people never…", "Wealthy families…").
- ❌ **Tax-evasion framing** ("pay $0 in taxes", "legal tax fortress"). Vance may explain what a rule is (e.g. the $10,000 cash reporting rule, what a revocable trust does), with a source link and "not tax or legal advice".
- ❌ **Fake testimonials and fake "sale" prices** in the store.
- ❌ **Misleading facts.** "Rich people never deposit over $10,000" hints at *structuring* deposits to avoid the $10K report, which is itself illegal (31 U.S.C. 5324). If Vance uses this hook, the payoff must be the truth: *why* the $10K report exists and why splitting deposits to dodge it is a crime.
- ✅ **Keep what she already does right:** the "AI content" label, which didn't stop 418K views.
